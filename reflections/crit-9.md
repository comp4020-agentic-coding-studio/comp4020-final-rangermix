# Crit 9 reflection

**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

The breakthrough this week was a second pair of eyes that hadn't built the code. After each phase, fresh reviewer agents read the work cold, and two skeptics tried to knock down every finding before it counted. They found what my tests hadn't, and most of it was about several people at once, which is what this crit is about: a reconnect that didn't count as being there, someone able to carry two pieces of furniture, and a half-cancelled grab that broke "first grab wins". Closing crit 8's cut list also turned a "fluke" into a real bug: everyone walked in to the same tile, so a newcomer hid behind whoever was standing there.

The reviews also showed me the limit of my setup. Phase 3's came back with 34 findings, 10 of them major, all at once. So I changed the workflow: the rest is built in small vertical slices, each with its own spec and a plan written just before it's built, so a review comes after every slice instead of every phase.

**What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?**

My prompts got shorter this week, sometimes just "/goal finish phase 3", because the direction was already written down: the plan, the list of what I'd cut for crit 8, the review findings. When the course budget ran out in the middle of phase 3, the next session picked up from those files where the last one stopped. I want to be the developer who builds that system around the work, with decisions, checks and handoffs in files, so I can step away and it still goes the way I meant.
